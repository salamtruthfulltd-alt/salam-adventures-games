For parents and carers

Supportive ideas for calmer, happier learning

You know your child best. Use these activities flexibly, keep expectations kind and stop before learning becomes overwhelming.

Keep sessions short

Five or ten positive minutes can build more confidence than pushing through tiredness. Finish while the activity still feels successful.

Offer choice and control

Let your child choose between two activities, select colours or decide whether to print or play. Small choices can reduce anxiety.

Make breaks part of learning

Movement, quiet time, a drink or sensory support are not interruptions. They help children regulate and return ready to learn.

Model without taking over

Show one example, think aloud and then give space. Gentle prompts support independence better than correcting every step.

Repeat without apology

Repetition builds familiarity and security. Returning to a favourite activity is valuable practice, not going backwards.

Notice the whole child

Learning includes communication, confidence, creativity, emotional regulation and everyday independence—not only right answers.

A gentle reminder: activities on Compass SEN Support are educational resources, not assessments or medical advice. Adapt them to your child, offer supervision where needed and seek professional guidance when you have concerns about development or wellbeing.

About Compass SEN Support

Compass SEN Support is a free family learning space where faith, imagination and education come together. It was created to make useful activities easier to find and to help children feel capable, included and excited to learn.

Helpful answers

Questions parents often ask

Are the games and generators free?

Yes. Activities are available for personal and educational use unless a page clearly states otherwise.

Are the resources suitable for children with additional needs?

Many activities use clear visuals, repetition, adjustable levels and short tasks that may support a wide range of learners. Every child is different, so choose and adapt resources based on individual needs.

How long should a child use an activity?

There is no fixed time. A short, positive session is often best. Watch for signs of tiredness or frustration and offer a break.

Can teachers and home educators use the worksheets?

Yes, the resources may be used for personal teaching and educational practice. They must not be resold or republished as another product.
